Understanding the Semiconductor Supply Chain: Why the Chip Shortage Isn't Over

📰 Source: US Market Live

The global semiconductor shortage, which disrupted industries from automotive to consumer electronics, has eased somewhat, but it is far from over. The complex supply chain and geopolitical tensions continue to pose risks.

Semiconductors are the backbone of the modern economy, powering everything from smartphones to cars. The production process is highly concentrated in a few regions, particularly Taiwan, South Korea, and China, making the supply chain vulnerable to disruptions.

Geopolitical tensions, particularly between the US and China, have led to export restrictions and tariffs, further complicating the industry. Both countries are investing heavily in domestic chip production, but building new fabrication plants takes years and billions of dollars.

For investors, the semiconductor sector remains highly attractive due to the long-term demand for AI and advanced computing. However, the cyclical nature of the industry and geopolitical risks require careful analysis. Companies with a strong technological moat and diversified supply chains are best positioned to thrive.
