Nasdaq Enters Correction Territory After a Severe Technology and Chip Sell-Off

📰 MARKETS | US Market Live
Share:

U.S. technology shares suffered one of their sharpest sessions in more than a year on November 5, as investors reassessed the cost, regulation and financing risks surrounding the artificial-intelligence infrastructure boom. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5%, while the S&P 500 lost 4.4% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 3.4%. The Nasdaq’s decline placed it more than 10% below its recent record closing level, the conventional threshold for a market correction.

Semiconductor stocks were among the hardest hit. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell about 12.2%, with major chipmakers and companies tied to data-center construction under pressure. Qualcomm also weakened after its outlook for smartphone chip sales disappointed investors. The declines reflected several concerns: valuations, the pace of capital spending and the possibility that political resistance could delay new data-center projects.

Recent election results and local votes opposing some data-center developments raised the prospect of tougher permitting conditions in important growth markets. That does not mean existing projects will automatically be cancelled. It does mean investors may demand clearer evidence that planned facilities can secure electricity, permits, customers and financing on schedule.

The sell-off is a reminder that a powerful long-term technology trend does not guarantee uninterrupted gains for every company associated with it. Investors are increasingly separating businesses with contracted demand and credible cash generation from those whose valuations depend on distant growth assumptions. The correction may create opportunities, but it also highlights the risk of treating the entire AI supply chain as one trade.
