U.S. Stocks Rally as Oil Slides and Investors Reassess AI Risks

Wall Street rebounded on November 9, with the S&P 500 rising 2.3% as lower oil prices and stronger corporate results helped restore risk appetite.

U.S. stocks staged a broad rebound on Monday, November 9, as falling oil prices and upbeat corporate earnings helped investors recover much of the previous week's losses. The S&P 500 advanced 2.3%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 2.2% and the Nasdaq Composite rose 2.7%, according to the Associated Press.

Major indexes post strong gains

The S&P 500 added 173.08 points to close at 7,549.94. The Dow climbed 1,104.02 points to 52,389.75, while the Nasdaq gained 673.71 points to 25,253.14. The Russell 2000 rose 3.1%, outperforming the larger benchmarks.

Energy markets provided part of the relief. Brent crude fell 5.8% to $92.57 a barrel amid hopes that diplomatic negotiations could reduce the risk of renewed disruption. Lower oil prices can ease pressure on transport costs and inflation expectations, although geopolitical developments remain uncertain.

AI investment remains in focus

Technology shares participated in the rebound, but investors continue to debate the scale and timing of spending on artificial-intelligence infrastructure. Questions about data-center power demand, project approvals and the returns on large capital investments remain relevant even after a strong trading session.

The rally marks a sharp improvement in sentiment, not proof that volatility has ended. Investors will continue to monitor inflation releases, Treasury yields, company guidance and developments affecting energy supply.
